## 1. Executive Summary

If you're reading this in August 2026, the situation in Palestine is worse than most people realize. What follows is not a simple story of war and peace. It's a story of multiple crises happening simultaneously, each one feeding into the others, creating a situation that is arguably the most complex humanitarian and geopolitical disaster in the world right now.

Here's the short version: Gaza has a "ceasefire" on paper since October 2025, but people are still being killed by airstrikes every week. The West Bank is experiencing record levels of settler violence. The Palestinian Authority is nearly bankrupt and functionally an extension of Israeli occupation infrastructure. A new organization called the "Board of Peace" — created by Donald Trump — is supposed to rebuild Gaza but has zero presence there and no money in its official accounts. The International Criminal Court has issued arrest warrants for Israeli leaders, but the U.S. is sanctioning the ICC instead of enforcing its rulings. An entire generation of Palestinian children is being denied education and traumatized beyond what traditional psychology can measure. And through it all, the arms industry is booming, reconstruction plans are being drawn up by the same business interests that profit from occupation, and the international community is fracturing between those who try to enforce international law and those who exempt their allies from it.

This report breaks down every angle — military, humanitarian, economic, legal, environmental, generational, and geopolitical — so you can understand not just what's happening, but why it matters and where it might be headed.

## 2. Gaza: The Ceasefire That Never Was

### The October 2025 Ceasefire Framework

In October 2025, a U.S.-brokered "Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict" was adopted under UN Security Council Resolution 2803. The plan established a ceasefire, secured the release of all hostages, and authorized an International Stabilization Force (ISF) to monitor compliance. It also endorsed the "Board of Peace" — chaired by Donald Trump — as a transitional governance administration for Gaza, and mandated Hamas' disarmament and IDF withdrawal.

On paper, this was a comprehensive roadmap. In reality, it has stalled at almost every phase.

### Military Reality on the Ground

Despite the ceasefire, military activity has continued unabated. According to Gaza's Ministry of Health, **1,259 Palestinians have been killed and 4,148 injured** since the ceasefire announcement in October 2025. Israeli airstrikes in August 2026 alone have killed civilians, including children — most recently at a café in western Gaza City where at least seven Palestinians, including a child, were killed.

The core deadlock is simple: Hamas refuses to disarm, citing Israeli breaches of the ceasefire and continued restrictions on humanitarian aid. Meanwhile, the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG) — a body of Palestinian technocrats established under Board of Peace oversight — has been unable to even enter Gaza, though it has prepared administrative frameworks and vetted civilian police candidates.

A 15-point roadmap presented by the guarantor states (Egypt, Qatar, Turkey, and the U.S.) demands "one authority, one law and one weapon" — meaning Hamas must cease all military, policing, and administrative activity. Talks on this roadmap remain unresolved.

### Hamas: Battered But Not Broken

Despite Israeli claims of having killed over 22,000 Hamas operatives, the organization's military wing — the Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades — has restabilized under acting commander Izz al-Din al-Haddad. Post-ceasefire IDF assessments estimated Hamas retained roughly **20,000 operatives**, down from 30,000 pre–October 7, 2023, but with new recruits of reportedly lower quality and limited training.

Critically, Hamas still possesses **hundreds of rockets** — mostly short-range, but some capable of reaching central Israel — along with thousands of light weapons. It operates as a guerrilla force in small units. Some units in the central Strip were barely touched because the IDF avoided those areas to prevent hostage harm.

Hamas has refused to disarm, citing Israeli breaches of the October 2025 ceasefire and continued aid restrictions. It has also blocked Board of Peace–approved contractors from entering Rafah to build temporary shelters, treating reconstruction as a political weapon.

### The IDF: Occupation by Another Name

Israeli forces still control **approximately 64% of Gaza**, with Netanyahu pushing to expand that to 70%. The "ceasefire" has not stopped military operations. The International Stabilization Force (ISF) — envisioned as peacekeepers under the ceasefire plan — has effectively collapsed. Indonesia, which pledged 8,000 troops, suspended participation after U.S./Israeli strikes on Iran in February 2026. No meaningful international military presence exists on the ground.

## 3. The Humanitarian Catastrophe in Gaza

### Food Insecurity: Fragile Gains, Dire Projections

While humanitarian access improved after the October 2025 ceasefire, the situation remains critical:

- **1.2 million people (59% of the population)** faced Crisis-level food insecurity or worse between April–June 2026.
- The IPC projects this will rise to **1.4 million (67%)** between July–December 2026 due to funding shortfalls, aid coverage declines, and expanding militarized zones.
- Inflation in Gaza has hit **209%** compared to pre-October 2023 levels.

### Water and Sanitation Collapse

- **90% of Gaza's population** experiences moderate-to-high water insecurity as of July 2026, up from 84% in June. Gaza City is the worst affected at 92%.
- Freshwater supplies are severely limited and heavily polluted. The collapse of sewage treatment infrastructure and use of cesspits have contaminated Gaza's aquifer. Marine and coastal areas are likely contaminated.
- Acute watery diarrhea has increased **36-fold**; acute jaundice syndrome (indicative of hepatitis A) has increased **384-fold**.

### Healthcare: A System in Full Collapse

The healthcare system is in full collapse. A UN protection brief from August 2026 describes it as "A System in Crisis."

- WHO has recorded **45 attacks on healthcare** in Gaza in 2026 alone, nearly half in Rafah.
- **Every fourth Palestinian injured** since October 2023 is estimated to live with spinal cord or brain injuries.
- The healthcare system cannot handle the volume of trauma cases, amputations, and chronic disease management.

### Environmental Annihilation: An Uninhabitable Strip

The UNEP's September 2025 assessment paints a picture of ecological collapse:

- **Agriculture destroyed:** Gaza has lost **97% of tree crops, 95% of shrubland, and 82% of annual crops**. Food production at scale is no longer possible.
- **Debris mountain:** **78% of Gaza's estimated 250,000 buildings** have been damaged or destroyed, generating **61 million tonnes of debris** — equivalent to 15 Great Pyramids of Giza by volume. About 15% may be contaminated with asbestos, industrial waste, or heavy metals.
- **Soil destruction:** Military activity has compacted soil, reducing water absorption, increasing flood risks, and diminishing groundwater recharge.

### Displacement and Explosive Hazards

Most of Gaza's population remains internally displaced. Since October 2023, **589 explosive ordnance incidents** have killed 274 people and injured 1,142 — including a 13-year-old child injured by abandoned ordnance in early August 2026.

Nearly all of Gaza's current population of 2.1 million people has been displaced, lacking access to sufficient shelter, food, life-saving medical services, clean water, education, and livelihoods.

## 4. The West Bank: A Parallel Crisis

### Record-Level Settler Violence

2026 has seen **unprecedented settler attacks** in the occupied West Bank. Al Jazeera reports they have reached **record levels**, with violence, arson, and fighting causing widespread displacement.

Just in the first ~20 weeks of 2026, OCHA documented **~680 settler attacks** across over 200 communities — an average of **six incidents per day**.

### Recent Incidents (August 2026)

- **Qusra Siege:** Israeli settlers have surrounded several Palestinian homes in Qusra (south of Nablus) for over a week, blocking residents from leaving. A Palestinian-American, Loui Ridi, flew from Ohio to defend his family home. U.S. Ambassador Mike Huckabee called the siege a "horrific act of terror."
- **Masafer Yatta:** On 5 August, ~30 settlers attacked Tuba community, injuring five Palestinians (including two girls), destroying three homes by fire, and displacing 23 people (14 children). A solar power system was destroyed, leaving ~100 residents without electricity.
- **Qalandiya Camp:** A large-scale Israeli military operation injured 51 Palestinians and disrupted health services. Nine structures were demolished nearby, affecting 71 people.
- **38 settler attacks** were reported in a single week in early August, resulting in casualties and property damage.

### Palestinian Authority: A Zombie State Being Strangled

The Palestinian Authority (PA) is nearly insolvent. It faces a deficit exceeding **4.5 billion shekels (~$1.9 billion)**. Civil servants receive only **60% of their salaries**. Schools operate just three days a week. Ministerial offices in Ramallah sit mostly empty.

This is not accidental. Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich has openly stated: "As far as I'm concerned, let the [Palestinian] Authority collapse. It is an enemy." Israel's withholding of Palestinian tax revenues — combined with the loss of tens of thousands of Palestinian jobs in Israel since October 2023 — has engineered a structural deficit that dates back to 2018.

The PA survives only through **EU and World Bank emergency funds**; Arab state contributions are minimal.

### Security Coordination: The Occupation's Subcontractor

The PA Security Forces (PASF) — nearly 30,000 armed personnel — function primarily as **Israel's security subcontractor in the West Bank**. Their core missions, developed under U.S. tutelage, are: taking armed gunmen off the streets, running a strict anti-Hamas campaign, and coordinating with Israeli forces.

This coordination includes intelligence sharing, counterterrorism targeting of Hamas and Islamic Jihad, deconfliction during IDF raids into Area A, and riot control to prevent demonstrations from reaching seam zones.

The result is a force that protects Israeli settlers and suppresses Palestinian resistance while being unable to protect Palestinian civilians from settler violence. As one Bethlehem civil society leader put it, the PA has become "a security sub-contractor...the job of the Palestinian security forces is to enforce the occupation on Israel's behalf."

### Demolitions and Displacement

- In a single week in August, Israeli authorities demolished **25 Palestinian-owned structures** in eight incidents, displacing 29 people (including 17 children). This included part of a school in Masafer Yatta affecting 54 students.
- In 2026, over **3,200 Palestinians have been displaced** across the West Bank by settler attacks and demolitions for lack of Israeli-issued building permits — an average of **17 people per day**, double the daily rate over the preceding three years.
- In Hebron's H2 area, Israeli forces installed a gate that restricts one of the last remaining access routes to the Old City, further expanding an increasingly institutionalized system of movement restrictions affecting about 7,000 Palestinians.

### Healthcare Under Siege in the West Bank

- WHO recorded **98 attacks on healthcare** in the West Bank between January–July 2026, including 37 in July alone (compared to 8 in June).
- Attacks affected 83 health transport vehicles and 11 facilities, causing 33 injuries and 4 fatalities.
- Palestinian Ministry of Health facilities face reduced hours and shortages of medicines due to the prolonged withholding of Palestinian clearance revenues by Israel.

## 5. The Generational Catastrophe: A Lost Generation

### Education Decimated

**97% of schools in Gaza** have sustained damage. **91.8% of school buildings** (518 of 564) need full reconstruction or major rehabilitation to function.

UNRWA warned that if the war dragged into 2026, Palestinian children could lose **up to five years of education**. Combined with daily trauma, this risks creating "a lost generation of permanently traumatised Palestinian youth."

At least **39,300 children had lost parents** and over **650,000 had no access to schooling** as of early 2026.

### Mental Health: Beyond PTSD

The mental health crisis in Gaza has reached levels that defy traditional diagnostic categories. Mental health experts consider the traditional Post Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) insufficient due to the lack of a "post-trauma" environment. They instead use **chronic traumatic stress disorder (CTSD)** to diagnose a population facing prolonged indefinite bombardment and displacement.

Key findings from peer-reviewed studies:

- A January 2026 study of 933 displaced children (average age 7.6 years) found:
  - Children experienced an average of **6.7 forced displacements** and **6.6 out of 10 war-related exposures**
  - Nearly all experienced hunger (98%) and house destruction (95%)
  - **57.8% met criteria for probable PTSD**
  - Over **46.3% classified as abnormal** on emotional and behavioral problem scores

- A December 2025 longitudinal study found the proportion of adults with high psychological distress **more than tripled to 67.2%** by early 2025 — twelve times higher odds than pre-war levels.

- A January 2026 cross-sectional study of 788 adult survivors found:
  - **82% moderate-to-severe depression**
  - **Over 20% reported suicidal ideation near-daily**
  - Those who fled Gaza reported significantly higher depression rates (90.9%) than those who remained (72.6%)

- By 2024, almost all of Gaza's 1.2 million children required urgent mental health and psychosocial support — a crisis UNICEF noted had never been reached anywhere in the world.

- **96% of children** living through the war felt their death was imminent, and nearly half reported wanting to die in November 2024.

Clinicians treated children who stopped speaking, stopped crying, or developed speech problems. Some children played games called "air strike," re-enacted bombings with stones, and played pretend death and burial.

Nisreen Qawas, a psychologist with the Palestine Red Crescent Society, called the crisis a "psychological injury of an entire generation."

### Children in the West Bank

In the West Bank, children face a different but equally brutal reality. Israeli soldiers at checkpoints have forced children at gunpoint to deny their Palestinian identity, to say "Long live Israel," and to draw the Star of David on their hands.

Settlers vandalize schools at night — smashing desks, tearing posters, scattering books. One 12-year-old girl described her school after an attack: "Torn and scattered on the floor, nothing was in its place...Life changed, my grades dropped, and I couldn't study anymore. When I try to study, I imagine them standing in front of the window."

## 6. The Economic War: Who Profits from Destruction

### The Israeli Arms Industry Boom

While Gaza is destroyed, Israel's defense sector is thriving. Israeli weapons sales **more than doubled over the past five years**, hitting a record of nearly **$15 billion in 2024**. For the first time, Israel surpassed the UK to become the **world's seventh-largest arms exporter**.

The logic is perverse: foreign arms purchases sustain Israeli production lines that can be redirected to domestic use in wartime. When war broke out in October 2023, Israeli manufacturers diverted **$1.5 billion worth of weapons** originally meant for foreign buyers to the IDF.

The UK — despite political tensions — saw imports of Israeli military goods jump **over 10,000%** between 2023 and 2025, from $74,517 to nearly $8 million.

### Gaza Reconstruction: Disaster Capitalism

The Board of Peace's Economic Development Plan positions **international corporate actors at the heart of Gaza's recovery** — without Palestinian consultation. A proposed Special Economic Zone (SEZ) would offer tax-free, tariff-free incentives to foreign investors, creating what critics call an "enclave economy with low-paid jobs, divorced from national workers' protections."

The UN database of business involvement in the Occupied Palestinian Territory lists **158 enterprises** supporting the occupation in construction, real estate, and mining. The UN Special Rapporteur identified **48 corporate entities** from arms, technology, oil/gas, and construction sectors that have "profited from Israel's economy of illegal occupation...and genocide."

Jared Kushner's **$30 billion "master plan"** for Gaza — 180 skyscrapers, coastal tourism zones, 100,000 housing units in Rafah — sits uneasily alongside his private equity firm Affinity Partners managing billions in Gulf capital. The New Yorker reports the Trump family has monetized the presidency to the tune of **$4 billion**.

Meanwhile, the World Bank estimates Gaza reconstruction will cost **$53–70 billion**. The Board of Peace pledged $17 billion; as of May 2026, **$0 had reached official accounts**.

### Palestinian Economy in Ruins

The Palestinian economy has been devastated:

- Recovery and reconstruction needs in Gaza are estimated at **$71.4 billion over the next decade**, including $26.3 billion in the first 18 months.
- The conflict has set back **human development in Gaza by 77 years**.
- Unemployment reached **46% in 2025** — 28% in the West Bank and **78% in Gaza**.
- Nearly **three quarters of Gaza's pre-October 2023 employed population** have lost their jobs, resulting in an employment-to-population ratio of just 9.3%.
- Overall prices in the Occupied Palestinian Territory increased by approximately **11% in 2025**, driven by a sharp increase in Gaza where prices rose by around 22% and the food consumer price index increased by 71%.
- By February 2026, prices in Gaza remained **154.7% above pre-October 2023 levels**.
- In the West Bank, employment in Israel and Israeli settlements fell from approximately **172,000 workers before October 2023 to 51,000** by Q4 2025.
- The PA's total public debt stood at **$14.6 billion by end of 2025**, equivalent to **106% of 2024 GDP**.
- Israel was withholding approximately **$4.4 billion in clearance funds** by early 2026.

## 7. The Board of Peace: Trump's Alternative to the UN

### What It Is (and What It Isn't)

The **Board of Peace (BoP)** is an international organization established by President Donald Trump, formally ratified at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, in January 2026. It was created as part of the U.S.-brokered 20-point "Comprehensive Plan to End the Gaza Conflict," endorsed by UN Security Council Resolution 2803 in November 2025.

But here's the critical thing: **the Board of Peace's charter does not mention Gaza, Israel, Hamas, or Palestinians.** What began as a Gaza-specific mechanism has been expanded — by design — into a **permanent global peace organization** with ambitions that extend far beyond the Strip.

### Structure: A Trump-Centric Hierarchy

The BoP operates on multiple tiers, all orbiting Trump:

**Chairman for Life:** Donald Trump is explicitly named in the charter as chairman, with no term limits. He holds the **sole authority** to invite countries, create or dissolve subsidiary entities, approve all charter revisions, and nominate his own successor. He has stated he intends to remain chairman for life, independent of his U.S. presidency.

**The Executive Board (7 members):** Appointed by Trump:
- Marco Rubio — U.S. Secretary of State
- Steve Witkoff — U.S. Special Envoy to the Middle East
- Jared Kushner — Trump's son-in-law
- Tony Blair — Former UK Prime Minister
- Marc Rowan — CEO of Apollo Global Management
- Ajay Banga — President of the World Bank Group
- Robert Gabriel Jr. — American political advisor

**The Gaza Executive Board:** The operational arm for Gaza specifically, including Turkish, Qatari, Egyptian, and UAE officials, plus Nickolay Mladenov as High Representative for Gaza.

**The Full Board:** Countries can buy **permanent membership for $1 billion**, or serve three-year terms. As of 2026, 25 of 62 invited countries have signed the charter. Notable members include Argentina's Javier Milei, Hungary's Viktor Orbán, and Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu.

**No other G7 state has joined.** Canada, France, Germany, and Spain declined. Norway, Sweden, and France publicly refused.

### The Charter's True Ambition: Replacing the UN?

The BoP's charter frames it as "an international organization that seeks to promote stability, restore dependable and lawful governance, and secure enduring peace in areas affected or threatened by conflict" — **globally**, not just in Gaza.

It explicitly laments that "too many approaches to peace-building foster perpetual dependency, and institutionalize crisis," positioning itself as "a more nimble and effective international peace-building body" than the UN.

Trump has stated the BoP could **potentially replace the UN**, while also expressing support for the latter. This coincides with the U.S. withdrawing from 66 international organizations in January 2026, including 31 UN bodies.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot put it bluntly: "Yes to implementing the peace plan... but no to creating an organization as it has been presented, which would replace the United Nations."

### The Money Problem: Pledges vs. Reality

The funding picture is deeply problematic:

- **$17 billion pledged** ($10 billion from the U.S., $7 billion from others) at the February 2026 inaugural meeting.
- **$0 in the official World Bank account** as of May 2026.
- Donations have instead flowed into a **private J.P. Morgan account with no oversight**.
- A BoP official admitted to The Hill they had collected only **"hundreds of millions of dollars"** — far short of pledges.
- Secretary Rubio confirmed **no U.S. funds have actually flowed** to the accounts.

Sen. Catherine Cortez Masto introduced legislation to block $1 billion in State Department funds from going to the BoP, calling it a **"slush fund."**

The World Bank estimates Gaza reconstruction alone will cost **$53–70 billion**. The BoP's pledged $17 billion was already insufficient; the fact that even that hasn't materialized makes the funding gap catastrophic.

### Operational Reality: A Body That Can't Operate

Despite its grand charter, the BoP is **functionally paralyzed** in Gaza:

- The **National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG)** — the 15-member Palestinian technocrat body meant to govern Gaza day-to-day — **has never entered Gaza**. It is based in Cairo.
- Hamas has **blocked BoP-approved contractors** from entering Rafah to build temporary shelters.
- The **International Stabilization Force (ISF)** — meant to serve as peacekeepers — has only one firm troop commitment (Indonesia pledged 8,000), but Indonesia **suspended participation** after U.S./Israeli strikes on Iran in February 2026.
- Israeli forces still control **~64% of Gaza**, with Netanyahu pushing to expand to 70%.

Rubio admitted to Congress: "There are other elements of the 20-point plan that are ongoing… It's just, you can't implement it. No one's going to invest money in Gaza until Hamas is demilitarized because they know there's going to be another war."

### The Reception: Vanity Project or Global Threat?

Critics have been scathing:

- **The Guardian** called it "a Trump-dominated pay-to-play club: a global version of his Mar-a-Lago court aimed at supplanting the UN itself."
- **Financial Times**: "a fledgling club of autocrats"
- **Sydney Morning Herald**: "vanity project"
- **German officials**: described it as a "counter-draft" to the UN.
- **EU Institute for Security Studies**: warned it risks "normalizing parallel structures that centralize power while weakening multilateral legitimacy."

Ahmed Fouad Alkhatib of the Atlantic Council assessed it bluntly: "There is a total disconnect between the mandate of the Board of Peace and operationalizing it on the ground... And with no administrative or organizational infrastructure, the Board will never succeed or function."

## 8. The Legal War: ICC Warrants vs. U.S. Sanctions

### The ICC Strikes Back

On November 21, 2024, the ICC issued arrest warrants for **Benjamin Netanyahu** and **Yoav Gallant** for:
- **War crimes:** Starvation as a method of warfare; intentionally directing attacks against civilians
- **Crimes against humanity:** Murder, persecution, and other inhumane acts

The Court rejected Israel's jurisdictional challenges, ruling that Palestine's territorial jurisdiction — extending to Gaza and the West Bank — was sufficient.

### The ICJ Advisory Opinion

On July 19, 2024, the International Court of Justice rendered its landmark Advisory Opinion on the Legal Consequences arising from the Policies and Practices of Israel in the Occupied Palestinian Territory. The Court affirmed by a strong majority that:

- Israel's continued presence in the occupied Palestinian territory is **unlawful and must end as rapidly as possible**
- Israel must **immediately cease new settlement activities** and **evacuate all settlers**
- Israel must **make reparation for the damage caused**
- **All States** are under an obligation **not to recognize as lawful** the situation arising from Israel's unlawful presence
- All States must **refrain from rendering any aid or assistance** in maintaining the unlawful situation

Two years later, in July 2026, this finding has gone largely unheeded.

### The U.S. Counter-Attack

The Trump administration has responded by **sanctioning ICC officials**. On August 19, 2026, it imposed sanctions on **ICC President Tomoko Akane** and senior prosecutor **Abdoulaye Seye** — the latter involved in seeking arrest warrants for Netanyahu and investigating illegal settlement funding.

This follows the broader U.S. withdrawal from 66 international organizations in January 2026, including 31 UN bodies.

The message is clear: international law applies to everyone except America's allies.

## 9. Regional Power Chess

### Iran and Hamas: The Patron Relationship

Iran remains Hamas's primary patron. According to U.S. State Department reports, Iran historically provided about **$100 million annually** to Palestinian militant groups; by 2023, Israeli sources estimated this had risen to **$350 million annually**.

The regional war has altered the strategic calculus. Tensions with Iran are escalating concurrently. In August 2026, unidentified projectiles struck two ships in the Strait of Hormuz, and the UAE tracked ballistic missiles fired from Iran. President Trump posted a map of the strait labeled "new U.S. territory." Iranian officials claim the U.S. is "begging to talk."

### The Abraham Accords: Peace for Whom?

The Accords have proven institutionally resilient despite the Gaza war, but their expansion has stalled. In May 2026, Trump demanded that Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Pakistan, and Turkey sign the Accords, and even suggested **Iran should join**.

The reality: the Accords sidelined the Palestinian issue to build an anti-Iran alliance. They have not brought peace — they have **emboldened Israeli expansionism** while public opinion in signatory states remains overwhelmingly opposed.

In Saudi Arabia, **96% of respondents** in a 2023 poll believed Arab nations should cut ties with Israel. The gap between regime-level normalization and popular sentiment is a pressure cooker.

### Saudi Arabia's Position

Saudi Arabia — once the prize accession for the Abraham Accords — now views **both Iran and Israel as equally destabilizing forces**. Riyadh has repeatedly stated: **"No ties with Israel without a Palestinian state."**

### Egypt's Role: The Rafah Crossing

The Rafah border crossing with Egypt reopened on February 1, 2026, for limited traffic — a key step as the ceasefire moves ahead. However, the reopening is largely symbolic:

- Only **50 Palestinians cross in each direction per day** initially
- No goods allowed to cross at first
- About **20,000 Palestinian children and adults** needing medical care hope to leave
- Israel and Egypt vet all travelers; EU border patrol agents supervise
- Egypt has prepared **150 hospitals** to receive Palestinian patients

Egypt fears Israel could use the crossing to push Palestinians out of the enclave. Historically, Rafah was the main crossing for people moving in and out of Gaza. The territory's handful of other crossings are all shared with Israel.

### Qatar and Turkey's Mediation

Qatar and Turkey have emerged as key mediators. On August 3, 2026, they issued a joint statement with Egypt condemning ongoing Israeli violations in Gaza, particularly targeting of healthcare facilities.

The mediators stress the need for Israel to comply with international law and uphold ceasefire commitments. They warn that continued violations threaten the path toward de-escalation and exacerbate civilian suffering. They call on the international community to exert pressure on Israel to fulfill its obligations.

Qatar has a long history of mediating between Israel and Hamas, dating back to 2012. Turkey has strengthened its partnership with Qatar across political, economic, energy, and military cooperation.

### Jordan and Lebanon: The Spillover

In Jordan, Palestine refugees without citizenship — including many ex-Gazans and refugees from Syria — remain marginalized and heavily dependent on humanitarian assistance due to limited access to employment, documentation, and essential services.

In Lebanon, continued socioeconomic hardship, rising violence in camps, and restrictions on legal status continue to entrench multi-generational exclusion, with **more than 80% of Palestine refugees living below the poverty line**.

## 10. The Information War

### Targeting Journalists

Israel has systematically targeted journalists and media infrastructure. The numbers are staggering:

- In the first half of 2026 alone, Israel killed **8 Palestinian journalists** and committed **467 violations** against media workers in the West Bank and Gaza.
- This brings the total number of Palestinian journalists killed by Israel since October 8, 2023, to **264**.
- Over **4,127 violations** against Palestinian journalists were documented between October 2023 and end of June 2026, including the arrest of 193 journalists and the destruction of 187 media institutions and 140 journalists' homes.
- **713 relatives of journalists** were also killed during the same period.

The Committee to Protect Journalists has called this "the deadliest for journalists ever recorded."

### The Pattern

More than 41% of violations involved preventing media coverage or detaining press crews (192 cases). The Palestinian Journalists Syndicate described this as reflecting "a systematic policy aimed at obscuring the media landscape in the occupied Palestinian territories."

Israeli forces also barred 19 journalists from the Al-Aqsa Mosque compound, seized or damaged media equipment in 22 cases, raided seven homes and media organizations, closed three media institutions, and blocked five websites.

France's national union of journalists informed its Palestinian counterpart that it intended to file a lawsuit with French courts over war crimes against journalists.

### Narrative Control and Its Limits

The Palestinian crisis is fought on multiple battlefields, and the information domain is among the most contested. Israel has systematically targeted journalists and media infrastructure — killing Palestinian journalists, destroying broadcasting facilities, and restricting international press access. Simultaneously, a global network of online influencers, AI-generated content farms, and political action committees has worked to reframe the narrative.

Yet the information war has not been won. The sheer volume of citizen-generated content from Gaza — livestreams from bombed hospitals, voice notes from buried children, satellite imagery of erased neighborhoods — has created a permanent archive of atrocity that no amount of narrative management can fully suppress. The ICC warrants, the ICJ genocide case, and the growing movement for Boycott, Divestment, and Sanctions (BDS) all testify to the limits of information control.

The Trump administration's sanctions on the ICC represent not strength but fear — the fear that documented evidence, accumulated over years, is reaching institutions capable of acting on it.

## 11. UNRWA and the Humanitarian System

### Funding Crisis

UNRWA — the UN agency responsible for Palestinian refugees since 1949 — is at a breaking point. Its acting commissioner-general, Christian Saunders, warned the UN General Assembly in July 2026:

"UNRWA's financial situation is untenable and the viability of our operations across the region is at stake."

Key numbers:
- **$100 million cash shortfall**
- Austerity measures valued at **$175 million in 2025** had staved off mass layoffs
- In January 2026, UNRWA was forced to cut service-delivery hours by **20%**
- Slashed salaries of many Palestinian staff
- Kept **15.5% of international posts vacant**

Saunders warned: "These severe austerity and cost-control measures are not sustainable in the long term and cannot continue indefinitely."

### 15.3 Palestinian Tax Revenue: Israel's Weapon of Financial Warfare

These revenues constitute **two-thirds of the PA's total income**.

#### The Theft Escalates

- **2019–2024:** Israel deducted approximately **$1 billion** (3.54 billion NIS) from PA tax money — claiming it was equivalent to payments the PA made to families of prisoners and martyrs

- **May 2025 onward:** Israel **fully suspended the transfer of ALL clearance revenues**
- **By end of 2025:** Withheld funds estimated at **$4.5 billion**
- **June 2026:** The Knesset passed a **LAW** making these deductions permanent legislation, no longer just ministerial decisions. Israel receives **3% of the total deducted revenues** (~380 million shekels / $102 million annually) just for the "service" of collecting Palestinian taxes

### 17.1 The Gaza Death Toll: The Official Numbers and the Hidden Numbers

#### What the Gaza Ministry of Health Reports

The Gaza Ministry of Health (MoH) is the primary source for casualty figures in Gaza. Its methodology is straightforward: it counts bodies that arrive at hospitals, morgues, and collection points, and it cross-references with family reports and field verification. For more than two years, Israeli officials dismissed these figures as "Hamas propaganda" and "not accurate."

Then, in January 2026, something remarkable happened: **Israel's own military accepted the Gaza Health Ministry's death toll as broadly accurate.** A senior Israeli security official briefed journalists that about **70,000 Palestinians had been killed** by Israeli attacks since October 2023 — excluding those missing. It was the first time Israel had publicly estimated the toll. 

By that same point, the Gaza Health Ministry said the direct toll had exceeded **71,660 people**, with at least **10,000 presumed buried in the rubble** of bombed buildings.

#### The Lancet Global Health Study: The 35% Undercount

In April 2026, The Lancet Global Health published a peer-reviewed study that fundamentally changed the debate about Gaza's death toll. The researchers — including Michael Spagat, Jon Pedersen, Khalil Shikaki, and others — conducted a **population-representative household survey** between December 30, 2024, and January 5, 2025. They surveyed **2,000 households across 200 primary sampling units**, documenting the vital status of 9,729 household members. 

Their findings were devastating:

- **75,200 violent deaths** (95% confidence interval: 63,600–86,800) between October 7, 2023, and January 5, 2025
- This represents approximately **3.4% of Gaza's pre-conflict population**
- **Women, children (under 18), and elderly (over 64) comprised 56.2% of violent deaths** — totaling 42,200 deaths
- **16,300 non-violent deaths** (malnutrition, disease, untreated conditions), of which 8,540 were excess deaths above pre-conflict projections
- The MoH figure for the same period (49,090 violent deaths) was **34.7% below** the study's central estimate 

This means that for every 100 deaths the Gaza Health Ministry recorded, there were approximately **50 more** that never made it to a hospital or morgue — buried in rubble, lost in chaos, or dying silently in places no one could reach.

An earlier Lancet study (2024) had found a similar pattern: **64,260 estimated deaths by June 2024** versus the official figure of 37,877 — a **41% undercount**. 

The Max Planck Institute independently estimated **78,318 killed by December 31, 2024** — consistent with the Lancet findings. 

#### What This Means

If the Lancet study's methodology is correct — and it was peer-reviewed by one of the world's most prestigious medical journals — then the true death toll in Gaza is not 73,000. It is likely **well over 100,000** when accounting for:

1. The 35% undercount identified by the Lancet survey (applying this ratio to current figures)
2. The **10,000+ bodies presumed buried in rubble** that may never be recovered
3. The **indirect deaths** from starvation, disease, and lack of medical care (8,540 excess non-violent deaths identified by Lancet in just the first 15 months)
4. The deaths since January 2025 that the study did not capture

When indirect deaths are included, the total could approach or exceed **150,000** — nearly 7% of Gaza's pre-war population.

### 17.8 The Combatant vs. Civilian Debate: Netanyahu's False Math

In January 2026, Prime Minister Netanyahu claimed in an interview with The Economist that the civilian-to-combatant ratio in Gaza was **"about one and a half to one"** — meaning 1.5 civilians killed for every 1 combatant. He claimed this was lower than Western-led conflicts like WWII, Afghanistan, and Iraq (which he said were 6:1 or 7:1). 

This claim is **untrue on every account**.

A comprehensive analysis by Action on Armed Violence (AOAV) found:
- The actual civilian-to-combatant ratio in Gaza is consistently **4:1 to 9:1** or even higher, depending on assumptions
- The higher figure (9:1) applies to confirmed deaths recorded by the Gaza Health Ministry
- The lower figure (4:1) still relies on highly conservative acceptance of Israeli military claims
- Even a **1:1 ratio** would signal grave disregard for civilian life
- Gaza's ratios **exceed those seen in Afghanistan, Iraq, or other Western-led conflicts**
- They even exceed crude aggregate ratios derived from **World War II**
- When likely undercounted violent deaths and indirect deaths from starvation and disease are included, the civilian toll rises further 

The analysis concluded: **"Israel's conduct in Gaza involves widespread and wholly disproportionate civilian loss, directly contradicting official claims and placing the campaign among the most civilian-destructive of recent decades."** 

#### Why the Discrepancy?

Israel's low ratio relies on two dubious assumptions:
1. **Counting all adult males as combatants** — The IDF has routinely classified any military-age male (roughly 16–60) killed in Gaza as a "combatant" unless proven otherwise. This is statistically absurd in a population where adult males comprise roughly 20% of the total.
2. **Ignoring indirect deaths** — Starvation, disease, and lack of medical care are not counted as "war deaths" in Israeli military accounting, even though they are direct consequences of military strategy (siege, infrastructure destruction, aid blockade).

The Lancet study corrected for this systematic bias and found that women, children, and elderly — clearly non-combatants — comprised **56.2% of violent deaths**. Adult males of combat age comprised the remaining 43.8%, but many of those were also civilians (doctors, journalists, teachers, fathers).
